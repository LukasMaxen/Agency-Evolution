# Acceler8rs Playbook

## Send-Time Learning from Kasper Zacho

When a lead requests availability or asks to schedule, never mention in the draft that you lack calendar access. If calendar slots are available, include them. If not, simply share the Calendly link with a warm line like 'Feel free to grab a time here' — do not reference or explain the absence of specific slots. Also avoid templated openers; keep scheduling replies natural and lightly personalized to the lead.

When sharing a calendar link, always also propose a couple of specific available time slots inline (e.g. 'I have Tuesday at 10am or Thursday at 2pm EST — or grab a time here: [link]'). Offering slots directly feels more human and actionable than a bare calendar link, and avoids the reply coming across as overly AI-generated or templated.

When drafting a reply to a contact who was referred by someone else (e.g. 'please get in touch with our CFO/colleague'), always open with explicit context explaining the chain: who originally received our outreach, that they passed the contact along, and why we are now reaching out to this new person. The recipient is hearing from us cold and needs to understand immediately how we got their name and why we are contacting them.

When a sell-side lead replies asking for more info, do NOT treat this like a standard scheduling reply or inbound lead. Do NOT use generic opener phrases like 'Appreciate you coming back' or vague CTA language like 'For the M&A and capital markets side, grab a time here.' This is a founder who has expressed interest in a potential acquisition — the reply needs to feel considered and human, not templated. Instead: open naturally in response to their specific message, briefly explain why we can't share details in writing (standard procedure — it protects both sides and keeps things confidential), express genuine enthusiasm about walking them through everything on a call, then close with the calendar link. Keep it warm, concise, and professional. No filler phrases, no hollow openers.
