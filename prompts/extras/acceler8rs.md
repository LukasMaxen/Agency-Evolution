# Acceler8rs Playbook

## Send-Time Learning from Kasper Zacho

When a lead requests availability or asks to schedule, never mention in the draft that you lack calendar access. If calendar slots are available, include them. If not, simply share the Calendly link with a warm line like 'Feel free to grab a time here' — do not reference or explain the absence of specific slots. Also avoid templated openers; keep scheduling replies natural and lightly personalized to the lead.

When sharing a calendar link, always also propose a couple of specific available time slots inline (e.g. 'I have Tuesday at 10am or Thursday at 2pm EST — or grab a time here: [link]'). Offering slots directly feels more human and actionable than a bare calendar link, and avoids the reply coming across as overly AI-generated or templated.

When drafting a reply to a contact who was referred by someone else (e.g. 'please get in touch with our CFO/colleague'), always open with explicit context explaining the chain: who originally received our outreach, that they passed the contact along, and why we are now reaching out to this new person. The recipient is hearing from us cold and needs to understand immediately how we got their name and why we are contacting them.

When a sell-side lead replies asking for more info, do NOT treat this like a standard scheduling reply or inbound lead. Do NOT use generic opener phrases like 'Appreciate you coming back' or vague CTA language like 'For the M&A and capital markets side, grab a time here.' This is a founder who has expressed interest in a potential acquisition — the reply needs to feel considered and human, not templated. Instead: open naturally in response to their specific message, briefly explain why we can't share details in writing (standard procedure — it protects both sides and keeps things confidential), express genuine enthusiasm about walking them through everything on a call, then close with the calendar link. Keep it warm, concise, and professional. No filler phrases, no hollow openers.

When replying to an interested lead: (1) Never reference specific service verticals in the reply opener — do not say 'For the M&A and capital markets side' or any similar framing. (2) Ask for the lead's availability first, then share the Calendly link as a follow-up option — do not offer specific time slots since we do not have access to the sender's calendar. Do not write lines like 'Would either of the following times work' and then fail to list slots. (3) Always include the Calendly link in the reply regardless of how availability is asked about.

Never comment on what stood out about a lead's business or compliment their company unless they explicitly ask. Do not say things like 'I love what you're building' or 'what caught our attention about [company]' or any similar framing that editorializes about their business. Keep the focus on the purpose of the outreach. Also, never use the phrase 'For the M&A and capital markets side' in any reply — not as an opener, not inline, not anywhere.

When a lead replies with a casual/playful one-liner (e.g. 'Everybody's got a price'), match their light tone in the reply — friendly and soft, not overly jovial (avoid 'ha' or forced humour). Keep the reply short and concise: acknowledge their comment briefly, make one clear point about why a conversation is worth it, and close with the calendar link. Do not expand into multi-sentence category breakdowns.

Never write off or explicitly rule out the specific buyer mentioned in the outreach. Do not say things like 'the buyer who prompted this outreach isn't the right match' or fabricate details about a buyer's mandate that were never stated. Keep the reply soft, friendly, and human — acknowledge there could well be a fit with that buyer, mention that we work with a wide range of buyers so if that one isn't the right match there are likely others who could be, and offer a free informal valuation call with no strings attached. Let the lead feel the door is open, not that they have already been assessed and redirected. Never use the phrase 'For the M&A and capital markets side' anywhere in the reply.

When a lead asks if the buyer can move quickly: (1) Answer directly — yes, if the numbers align they can move fast. (2) Keep the reply short; do not over-explain the diligence process or speculate on specifics over email. (3) Never describe the lead's business or products using website research. The correct reply format is: confirm they can move quickly if the numbers align, add one short line redirecting to a call rather than speculating over email, then close with the Calendly link. Example: "Yes, if the numbers align they can move quickly. Best to walk through it on a call and see if there is a fit. Feel free to grab a time here: [link]"

Always close replies using only the {SENDER_EMAIL_SIGNATURE} variable — never write out a literal signature block alongside it, and never omit it. Use the variable every time, on its own, with no duplicate written-out name or title below it.

When including the Calendly link in replies, wrap it in angle brackets (e.g. <https://calendly.com/...>) rather than leaving it as a bare URL.

Never include both a written-out signature line (e.g. 'Lukas Maxen') AND the {SENDER_EMAIL_SIGNATURE} variable in the same draft. Always use {SENDER_EMAIL_SIGNATURE} alone — never the written-out block, never both together.

When a lead confirms a call or agrees to connect and asks for buyer details before the call, do not over-explain or list out the buyer's profile in full. Keep the reply brief and forward-moving: confirm the call is set, share one short line of context about the buyer (e.g. 'a private investment group focused on acquiring established consumer brands with committed capital ready to move'), then redirect remaining details to the call. Close with the Calendly link wrapped in angle brackets and {SENDER_EMAIL_SIGNATURE}.

When answering a simple factual question from a lead (e.g. 'which country is the buyer from?'), answer it directly and concisely in one short paragraph, then go straight to the CTA. Do not add a second paragraph explaining why you can't share more in writing — it adds friction and over-explains. Keep the reply tight: answer + CTA only.
