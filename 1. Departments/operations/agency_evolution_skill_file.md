## SECTION 2: USER PREFERENCES

### 2.1 Communication Style
- Direct, confident, no fluff — always
- Short over long — if in doubt, cut
- Never sounds like AI or a salesperson
- Conversational but professional
- No over-eager language
- Constructive corrections accepted; does not need excessive apology when corrected

### 2.2 Script/Email Formatting (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
- NO em dashes anywhere, ever
- NO bullet points in email scripts
- NO "last note from me" or final email signaling
- NO "genuinely," "honestly," "straightforward"
- NO "I'd love to," "I think," hedging language
- NO "Quick one" as an intro — specifically rejected
- NO "We're selectively bringing on X clients" as opener — must start with what you do
- NO "Is your agency accountable?" for DTC brands — they may not have an agency
- NO "at any one time" — use "at a time" or rephrase
- NO retainer mention unless prospect asks
- NO email links or URLs in cold outreach scripts
- Full signature always: Name, Title, Company (no email address, no links in scripts)
- P.S. opt-out for M&A/professional audiences only — never on consumer brand founders
- P.S. always: "Not of interest? Just let me know" (or variation)

### 2.3 Script Structure Preferences
- Step 1: Maximum length = 4 short paragraphs (example provided by user confirmed this)
- Step 2: Always opens "Following up in case of bad timing" (NOT "Reaching out again")
- Step 2: Structure always "Any interest in [best part of offer]?" — one question, no CTA
- Step 3: Always case study CTA (not call CTA) — lower friction
- Step 3: Opens "Reaching out in case my previous emails got buried" (NEVER "one last time")
- Three Step 1 variants required — must vary by 55%+ in length, format, wording
- Step 1 variants can include one question-led opener (but question must be very well-considered)
- Subject lines: all lowercase, 2-4 words optimal, spintax with 5 variations
- Spintax variations must not share same first word

### 2.4 Variable Rules by Client

- ITG: "Dear Sir" — NEVER {FIRST_NAME}
- GN Motion: {FIRST_NAME} and {COMPANY} standard

- All others: {FIRST_NAME} standard

## SECTION 3: LEARNED SKILLS

### 3.1 Cold Email Scripting

**Email 1 (Hook) — Rules:**
- Lead with what you do or a result — never with scarcity/selectivity
- One variant can open with a question — but must be highly targeted and non-generic
- Include a credibility signal: specific number, named client, documented result
- CTA must give a reason for the call — never just "quick call?"
- Maximum length confirmed by user example (4 short paragraphs)

**Email 2 (Interest Ping) — Rules:**
- Open: "Following up in case of bad timing"
- Structure: "Any interest in [most attractive part of offer]?"
- No CTA — the question IS the CTA
- Include a qualifying detail so only serious prospects reply
- Maximum 3-4 lines total

**Email 3 (Re-engagement) — Rules:**
- Open: "Reaching out in case my previous emails got buried"
- For consumer brands and DTC: case study CTA ("Mind if I send it over?")
- For M&A: teaser CTA or valuation angle
- Never signal it is the final email
- More informative than step 2 — reintroduce context

**Structural Variation Rules:**
- Variant A: Short (4 lines), single idea, no case study
- Variant B: Medium-long (7-9 lines), proof-led, full context, named results
- Variant C: Medium (5-7 lines), different angle (problem/solution, question lead, etc.)
- 55%+ difference in length, format, and wording between all variants

### 3.2 Subject Line Construction
- All lowercase (outperforms title case by 21% per data)
- 2-4 words optimal (46% open rate benchmark)
- 5 spintax variations minimum
- Variations must not share first word
- No marketing language (free, guaranteed, boost, results)
- No "quick question" — overused pattern flagged in 2026 data
- Curiosity gap and personal relevance are the two strongest drivers
- Examples that work: "a thought on your brand", "noticed your brand", "worth a look?", "your next 90 days"

### 3.3 GN Motion Vertical-Specific Hooks
- Tech: product specs, hero shots, feature visualization
- Jewelry: luxury presentation, light play, material texture
- Fashion: lifestyle, movement, texture
- Cosmetics: ingredient visualization, skin transformation, before/after
- All verticals: lead with complimentary sample, reason for sample differs by vertical
- L'Oreal Revitalift reference = use in Cosmetics campaign specifically

### 3.4 M&A Script Rules
- Never name the company being sold until NDA signed
- Use sector descriptor: "a [sector] business in [state/region]"
- Include revenue and EBITDA in step 1
- Remove $ and % when spam is flagged
- "No mandate, no fee" phrasing for Agency Evolution
- NDA framing for buyer identity questions: "standard practice in M&A to protect both sides"
- Pull all valuations/multiples to call — never confirm in email

### 3.5 Reply/Response Drafting
- Match length to prospect's message
- Short reply = short response (2-3 lines max)
- Objection = reframe as reason the call makes sense
- Not a fit = exit cleanly in 2-3 lines
- Redirect document requests to calls
- Always include correct calendar link for the sender
- Match language of correspondence (French reply = French email)

## SECTION 6: OPTIMIZATION STRATEGIES

### 6.1 When Scripts Stop Converting
1. Check for spam triggers: remove $, %, euro, tilde symbols first
2. Rotate visual structure — same paragraph count = same spam fingerprint
3. Change subject line spintax — ensure variations don't share first word
4. Shorten the script — most non-converting scripts are too long
5. Add qualifying detail to Step 2 — vague questions get curiosity replies not interest
6. Move strongest hook to line 1 — most founders decide in first sentence
7. Check if the offer has been market-tested — if interested rate is 0%, it is an offer problem not a copy problem

### 6.2 When Getting Replies But No Interested
- CTA is too easy to say yes to — add a qualifying filter
- Pre-qualify in the question so only serious prospects respond
- Example: "Any interest in working with a team that handles growth for consumer brands doing $500k+ per year?" filters better than "Any interest in a growth partner?"

### 6.3 When Search Volume Is Too Low
1. Switch industry filter to broader categories
2. Check OR vs AND logic on keywords
3. Drop employee minimum
4. Add adjacent geographies
5. Remove all keywords and let industry filter do the work

### 6.4 When Search Volume Is Too High/Noisy
1. Add targeted keywords (specific not vague)
2. Add exclude keywords for obvious noise categories
3. Raise employee minimum
4. Add a second industry filter to intersect categories

### 6.5 Deliverability Management by Volume
- Under 5k/month: standard 3-domain rotation
- 5k-10k/month: minimum 4-5 domains, weekly deliverability check
- 10k+/month: 6+ domains, spam trigger audit all scripts, subject line rotation mandatory, weekly bounce rate monitoring
- Bounce rate above 3% = list quality problem, pause and clean
- Unsubscribe rate above 0.5% = messaging problem or wrong audience
