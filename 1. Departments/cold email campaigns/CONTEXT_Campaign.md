# Campaign Context — Scripting & Execution Rules

*Cross-client rules for cold email scripting, sequence structure, spam avoidance, and lead search. Apply to all campaigns regardless of client.*

*Client-specific rules (hooks, CTAs, offer details, what works/doesn't) live in `clients/[slug].md`.*

---

## Formatting Rules (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

- **NO em dashes** — ever, under any circumstances
- **NO bullet points** in scripts
- **NO "last note from me"** or any language signaling it is the final email
- **NO P.S. opt-outs on cold consumer/DTC outreach** (Acceler8rs, Larsen Digital, GN Motion) — only use P.S. opt-outs for M&A, franchise, and professional audiences
- **NO "genuinely," "honestly," "straightforward"**
- **NO dollar signs or % symbols** when scripts are getting caught in spam — write out numbers in plain language
- **NO "capital solutions," "free," "guaranteed," "best," "amazing"** — common spam triggers
- **NO "I'd love to," "I think,"** or hedging language in CTAs
- **NO "Following up on [Company Name]"** as a step 3 opener
- **NO "once more," "last note," "final follow-up"** — signals end of sequence, kills reply rate
- **NO "just circling back"** — weak, ignored opener

---

## Tone

- Direct, professional, no fluff
- Conversational but not casual
- Confident without being arrogant
- Short over long — always err toward fewer words
- Never apologetic or defensive in framing

---

## Variable Usage

- For **eCommerce/DTC campaigns** (Acceler8rs, Larsen Digital): always use "your brand" — never {COMPANY}
- For **ITG**: always open with "Dear Sir" — never {FIRST_NAME}
- For **all other campaigns**: {FIRST_NAME} and {COMPANY} as normal
- For **M&A campaigns**: {COMPANY}, {CITY}, {STATE} — don't over-personalize; use only what feels natural
- For **UK campaigns**: no {STATE} variable — UK has countries and counties, not states

---

## Email Sequence Structure

### Step 1 — The Hook
- Lead with the most attractive part of the offer OR a recipient-focused opener
- Include a credibility signal (case study result, client name, revenue number, EBITDA)
- CTA by campaign type:
  - M&A sell-side: "Want me to send over the teaser?"
  - M&A buy-side / PE: "Would you be open to a short confidential call?"
  - CGI (GN Motion): "Worth a quick call to align on which product and concept would work best?"
  - Franchise (911 Restoration): "Worth a quick phone call?" — always specify PHONE CALL
  - eCommerce (Acceler8rs / Larsen Digital): "Want me to send over the case study?"
  - Investment (ZEBS): "Mind if I send over the teaser?"
  - AI / SaaS (Sonaro): "Free 30-day trial, worth a quick 15 minutes to see how it works?"
  - Industrial (ITG): qualifying question — "Which loom brand and model are you currently running?"

### Step 2 — The Follow-Up
- **Always open with:** "Following up in case of bad timing."
- **Always use:** "Any interest in..." as the question structure
- Keep it extremely short — one question, no pitch
- The question must include the most attractive part of the offer so only qualified people reply
- No separate CTA on step 2 — the question IS the CTA

### Step 3 — The Re-engagement
- Open with "Reaching out again in case my previous notes got buried" — never "following up on [name]"
- More informative than step 2 — reintroduce the offer with context
- Use a conversational tone that makes them feel like a peer
- CTA should give a specific reason for the call/action
- Never signal it is the last email

---

## CTA Rules

- Every call CTA needs a **reason** for the call — never just "quick call?"
- Good reasons: "to walk through what that could look like for your brand," "to align on which product and concept," "to see if it makes sense," "to see if there is a fit"
- Teaser/case study CTAs are lower friction than call CTAs — use when reply rate is the priority
- "Want me to send over the teaser?" beats "would you be open to a call?" for cold outreach
- For 911 Restoration: **always specify "phone call"** — never just "call" or "quick chat"

---

## P.S. Line Rules

- **M&A and professional audiences**: always include P.S. opt-out — busy executives want a clean out and it increases qualified replies
- **Consumer/DTC founders (Acceler8rs, Larsen Digital, GN Motion)**: P.S. opt-out primes them to say no — omit entirely
- **Franchise (911 Restoration)**: include P.S. but keep it simple — "If this isn't of interest, just let me know and I won't follow up."
- **Never use**: "just say the word and I'll leave you alone" on step 3 — signals end of sequence

---

## Spam Avoidance

- Rotate scripts every campaign cycle — same scripts get flagged
- Each variation must be **65–70% different** in wording AND visual structure
- Visual structure = paragraph count, line length, opener type, sentence rhythm
- Remove: $, %, €, £ symbols when scripts are getting caught in spam filters
- Remove: "free," "guarantee," "capital solutions," "rare opportunity"
- Replace "€300k+" with "300k+" etc.
- Subject lines must be spintaxed with at least 5 variations
- Subject line variations must NOT all share the same first word

---

## Spintax Subject Line Rules

- Mix: question format, statement format, brand-specific, generic
- Good example: `{Acquisition Opportunity|Premium Tequila Brand|{COMPANY} Opportunity|Spirits Brand Available|Tequila Brand Acquisition}`
- Bad example: `{Acquisition Opportunity|{COMPANY} Acquisition|Acquisition Opportunity {COMPANY}|Acquisition {COMPANY}|Acquisition {COMPANY} Opportunity}` — all contain "Acquisition," no real diversity

## Apollo / Lead Search Rules

### General Search Logic
- Industry filters do the heavy lifting — keywords are for refinement only
- "Include ALL" checkbox = AND logic (kills volume) — always use "Include ANY" (OR logic)
- Check "Type of Keywords" setting before launching — most failed searches were AND logic errors
- Company description checkbox adds noise — leave unchecked for includes, check for excludes
- Employee filter: PE firms run lean (10–50 people) — don't filter them out with high minimums

### Universal Exclude Keywords (Noise Filter)
Physical Therapy, Medical Clinic, Construction, Manufacturing, Heavy Industry, Software Development, Real Estate Brokerage, Non-Profit, Government, Chiropractic, Pharmaceutical, Logistics, Plumbing, HVAC, Excavation, Industrial, SaaS B2B, Biotech, Engineering, Architecture, Hospital, Dental, Veterinary, Automotive, Insurance, Staffing

### Title Batches for M&A (Ranked Best to Worst)
1. VP Corporate Development, Head of M&A, Director of Corporate Development
2. Investment Principal, Managing Partner, Investment Partner
3. VP Strategy, Director of M&A, Head of Strategy
4. M&A Director, Director of Investment, Director of Strategic Growth
5. Founder & CEO, Managing Director, Company Owner
6. Investment Vice President, VP Business Development, Chief Strategy Officer
7. President, Chief Operating Officer, Executive Vice President

Always attach department to seniority — "Investment Principal" not "Principal"

## What Never Works

1. Bullet points in scripts
2. Em dashes
3. "Last note from me" or any end-of-sequence language
4. "Capital solutions," "rare opportunity," "free" (use "complimentary" for GN Motion)
5. Leading with another brand's results without a recipient-focused hook first
6. "Your background in the trades is why I reached out" — overused, ignored (911 Restoration)
7. "I'd love to" — too soft
8. Padding a short effective step 2 with features — the question alone is enough
9. "Principal" alone as a title — always qualify: "Investment Principal"
10. Dollar signs or % symbols when spam is flagged

---

## What Always Works

1. Short emails outperform long ones — if in doubt, cut
2. Recipient-focused openers outperform result-first openers
3. Teaser/case study CTAs outperform call CTAs for cold outreach
4. Specific numbers ($6,342) outperform rounded numbers ($6k)
5. "Following up in case of bad timing" is the best step 2 opener
6. "Any interest in..." is the best step 2 question format
7. Including a qualifying detail in step 2 filters out unqualified replies
8. "Complimentary" outperforms "free" for spam avoidance
9. Named client social proof outperforms vague claims

## Campaign Performance KPIs

*Apply these thresholds before flagging or pausing any campaign. Do not make judgments on fewer than 1,000 emails sent.*

### Minimum Volume Gate
- **1,000 emails sent** before evaluating performance — any assessment before this is premature

### Core KPIs (both must be met)
| KPI | Threshold | How to calculate |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | ≥1% | Replies ÷ emails sent |
| Interested rate | ≥20% of replies | Interested replies ÷ total replies |

### Output targets (derived)
- ≤500 emails sent per interested lead
- ≤1,000 emails sent per booked meeting

### When a campaign fails KPIs
If a campaign has 1,000+ emails sent and misses either threshold:
1. **Flag the team** — add a `PERFORMANCE FLAG` note to the campaign row in the client file
2. **Investigate before pausing** — check deliverability, spam triggers, list quality, script structure first
3. **Pause if no fixable root cause** — update status to Paused in the client file and note the reason
4. **Never rebuild a failed campaign** without changing at least the audience, the angle, or both

### Do not apply KPIs to
- Campaigns with <1,000 emails sent (still in ramp phase)
- Mandate-specific outreach (e.g. single-company teasers) — evaluate by meeting booked, not reply rate
- Campaigns intentionally paused for strategic reasons unrelated to performance (e.g. list refresh, ICP pivot)

## Optimization

### When Scripts Stop Converting
1. Check if scripts are being caught in spam — remove $, %, €, ~ symbols first
2. Rotate visual structure — same paragraph count = same spam fingerprint
3. Change subject line spintax — ensure variations don't share first word
4. Shorten the script — most non-converting scripts are too long
5. Add a qualifying detail to step 2 — vague questions get curiosity replies, not interest replies
6. Move the strongest hook to line 1 — most founders decide in the first sentence

### When Getting Replies But No Qualified Interest
- The CTA is too easy to say yes to — add a qualifying filter
- Fix example: "Any interest in adding a 911 Restoration franchise?" → "Any interest in adding an insurance-backed revenue stream to your trade business in {CITY}? Our top 25% of franchisees average 2M+ in gross revenue."

### When Search Volume Is Too Low
1. Switch industry filter to broader categories
2. Check OR vs AND logic on keywords
3. Drop employee minimum
4. Add adjacent geographies (UK, Canada for US campaigns)
5. Remove all keywords and let industry filter do the work

### When Search Volume Is Too High / Noisy
1. Add targeted keywords (not vague ones like "portfolio company")
2. Add exclude keywords for obvious noise categories
3. Raise employee minimum
4. Add a second industry filter to intersect categories

### Script Variation Strategy (Spam Prevention)
- Vary: opener type (question / statement / result), paragraph count (2 vs 3 vs 4), line length pattern, CTA phrasing
- At least 65–70% different between variations
- Different subject line for each variation
- Test all variations simultaneously, not sequentially
