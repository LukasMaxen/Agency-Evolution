### 4. ICP Personas

_Three distinct personas for the sell-side campaign. Same offer, different framing. Each responds to different drivers, triggers, and language._

---

**Persona 1 — The Near-Retirement Owner**
- Who: 58–70 years old, founded or built the business over 15–25 years, no clear succession plan, kids aren't taking it over
- What they care about: Legacy, protecting the team they've built, not being rushed, confidentiality, not feeling like they're being sold to
- What resonates: A buyer who values what was built, a clean and controlled process, flexibility to stay involved if they want to, certainty of outcome
- What puts them off: Aggressive pressure, fee discussions early, anything that sounds like a hard pitch
- Lead angle: Legacy and timing — the right time to start understanding options is before you need to make a decision
- Real-world reference: Stellar Solar owners — 60s, ready to retire, motivated CFO. The CFO dynamic is worth noting: internal champions matter.

**Persona 2 — The Growth-Plateaued Operator**
- Who: 45–60, business is profitable and well-run but has hit a ceiling — needs capital, new distribution, or ownership scale to keep growing
- What they care about: Taking the business further, not just exiting. Finding a partner rather than a buyer. Financial security combined with continued relevance.
- What resonates: Partial sale options, staying involved post-deal, a buyer who can provide capital and operational scale, earn-out structures
- What puts them off: Being bought out completely with no say, losing the culture they've built, feeling like the business will be stripped
- Lead angle: Partnership framing — capital and support to reach the next level, not just an exit

**Persona 3 — The Never-Considered-Selling Owner**
- Who: Any age, business is running well, not actively looking, has never engaged with a buyer or advisor — this outreach is genuinely unexpected
- What they care about: Their business and team first, their own time second. Legitimacy of the approach. Not being wasted.
- What resonates: Curiosity. The seed that a conversation costs nothing. Proof that the buyer is credible and specific (not a blast). Knowing options exist without being pushed to act.
- What puts them off: Anything that sounds like a pitch, a generic template, or a broker fee structure
- Lead angle: Curiosity and options — not asking them to sell, just to understand what the market would say about their business today

### 7. Objections and Reframes

### 8. Language That Works / Doesn't Work

**What works**

| Type | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Personalisation | Revenue figures and EBITDA referenced by company in Step 1 | Shows research, not a blast. Triggers credibility immediately. |
| Geographic hook | {STATE} used in opener to ground the email in their location | Makes the email feel targeted, not national spray-and-pray |
| Partial sale framing | "A full exit isn't the only structure — partial sales and rollovers are on the table" | Removes the binary; opens the door for owners who assumed it was all-or-nothing |
| Confidentiality framing | "Everything stays confidential at this stage — no one is notified unless you decide to move forward" | Neutralises the fear of staff finding out, major unlock for long-tenured owners |
| Direct buyer clarity | "We're not a broker — we're representing the buyer directly. No fee on your side." | Differentiates from broker blast, removes cost concern immediately |
| Revenue/EBITDA in body | Include figures in Step 1, no dollar signs | Spam filter avoidance; shows specificity |

**What doesn't work**

| Type | Example | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Debt financing angle | Any messaging around debt restructuring or capital solutions | Underperforms sell-side by 4x on interested rate — dead angle, do not rebuild |
| Valuation in writing | Any attempt to estimate business value in an email | Breaks the rule, sets wrong expectations, removes incentive for a call |
| Naming the buyer | "Our buyer is [firm name]" or similar | Removes the confidentiality framing and the NDA rationale for the call |
| Generic subject lines | "Exciting opportunity for your business" or "Reaching out regarding a potential partnership" | Signals template immediately — deliverability and credibility both drop |
| Pressure language | "Limited window", "acting quickly", "before it's too late" | Wrong register for this audience — middle-market owners shut down at pressure |

**Objection Reframes**

| Objection | Reframe for FU |
|---|---|
| "Not for sale" | "Understood — most owners weren't actively considering it when we first spoke. Worth a call to understand options before you need to make a decision." |
| "Who's your buyer?" | "Standard practice at this stage is to keep the buyer confidential — it protects both parties before an NDA is signed. That's exactly what the first call is for." |
| "What's the valuation?" | "Too early to put a number on it without knowing more — giving you a figure without context would be doing you a disservice. The call addresses this directly." |
| "Only open to a partial sale" | "That's the majority of what we do. Partial sales and rollovers are fully on the table — a full exit isn't required." |
| "Send a proposal" | "Happy to send a brief overview. What would be most useful — deal structure options, or a sense of where comparable businesses in your sector have transacted?" |

---

**Break-Up Style**
Soft and professional. Leave the door genuinely open without any hint that it's a final attempt. No pressure language. No "this is my last email." The owner should feel they can re-engage at any point without awkwardness.

---

**FU Constraints**

- Never mention the number of follow-ups sent or reference the sequence
- Never name the specific PE buyer
- Never provide a valuation estimate in writing
- Never use dashes of any kind (em dash, en dash, hyphen as punctuation) in email body copy
- Never use colons in email body copy or before lists
- Never use dollar signs on revenue or EBITDA figures
- No pressure language — "limited window", "acting quickly", "before it's too late" are all wrong register
