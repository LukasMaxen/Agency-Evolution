### Client Type Reference

**Sell-Side M&A Advisor**
Represents business owners who want to sell. Cold outreach targets business owners as prospective sellers. The "product" is the advisory service — guiding an owner through the exit process, running a competitive process with buyers, and maximising the outcome. The prospect is not a buyer; they are being invited to consider an exit.

**Direct Buyer (PE / Strategic / Family Office)**
Approaches business owners directly with acquisition interest. No intermediary involved. The "product" is the acquisition itself — capital, a home for the business, and a deal that can happen quickly. The prospect is a business owner who may never have considered selling, or is curious about what their business is worth.

### 2. Psychological Drivers

_The emotional and rational forces that make this audience move. For each driver: what it means for this specific client's offer, the messaging angle that activates it, and the proof point or phrase that lands best._

---

**Sell-Side M&A Advisor — Primary Drivers**

| Driver | What it means | Messaging angle | Proof point / phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Financial Security** | Lock in value before the window closes. Market conditions change. The owner may only get one shot at a good exit. | Timing. Market is active now. Buyers are competitive. Delay = risk. | "Buyers are competing aggressively right now — owners who moved in the last 18 months achieved multiples well above historic norms." |
| **Legacy** | What happens to the business, the team, and the owner's name after they leave. This is often stronger than the financial driver for long-tenured owners. | Finding the right buyer, not just the highest bid. Protecting what they built. | "We run a process that surfaces buyers who want to grow what you've built — not strip it." |
| **Freedom / Optionality** | Exit = liquidity = choices. Relief from the day-to-day. Time back. Mental space. This resonates hardest with burned-out founders. | Life on the other side. What they get to do next. Not just what they're selling. | "Most of our clients say the biggest surprise is how different life feels 6 months after close." |
| **Fear of Missing the Window** | A peer just sold. Industry consolidation is starting. The owner feels the market moving and worries they'll miss it. | Now vs. later. What's at stake if they wait. | "We've seen three similar businesses in your sector go to market in the last 90 days. Competition for buyers narrows as supply increases." |
| **Distrust of the Process** | Fear of advisors, complexity, confidentiality breaches, wasted time, low offers. This objection needs to be neutralised before the pitch lands. | Transparency, confidentiality, no obligation until signed mandate. | "No signed mandate, no fee. And we don't tell your staff, customers, or suppliers until you decide you're moving forward." |
| **Control** | Will they be forced out? Can they stay involved? What happens to their team? Owners who built from zero fear losing control of the outcome. | They stay in control of the process. They choose the buyer. | "You decide when to go to market, who sees the business, and which offer to accept. We run the process — you make every decision." |

| Driver | What it means | Messaging angle | Proof point / phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity / Validation** | Receiving an approach makes the owner wonder what their business is actually worth. Even if not looking to sell, most owners are curious. | Plant the seed. No pressure. ### 4. ICP Personas

_2–4 distinct personas within the target audience. Same offer, different framing. Each persona responds to different drivers, triggers, and language. Use these to guide which angle to lead with per send._

---

**Sell-Side M&A Advisor — Personas**

**Persona 1 — The Near-Retirement Owner**
- Who: 58–68 years old, founded or bought the business 15–25 years ago, no clear successor
- What they care about: Legacy, protecting the team, not being rushed, confidentiality
- What resonates: Long-term buyer who values what was built, clean and controlled process, certainty
- What puts them off: Aggressive pressure, fee discussions too early, anything that feels like a hard sell
- Lead angle: Legacy and timing — "the right time to start thinking is before you need to"

**Persona 2 — The Burned-Out Founder**
- Who: 40–55, built the business from nothing, profitable but exhausted, wants freedom
- What they care about: Speed, simplicity, financial outcome, life after exit
- What resonates: Fast process, certainty of close, what life looks like on the other side
- What puts them off: Complexity, long drawn-out process, uncertainty
- Lead angle: Freedom and optionality — "you've built something valuable, time to benefit from it"

**Persona 3 — The Growth-Plateaued Operator**
- Who: 45–60, business is strong but has hit a ceiling — needs capital, new distribution, or scale
- What they care about: Taking the business further, financial security, finding the right partner
- What resonates: Buyer who can accelerate growth, partial sale options, staying involved post-deal
- What puts them off: Being bought out completely with no say, culture being destroyed
- Lead angle: Partnership framing — "a deal that takes the business to the next level, not just an exit"

**Persona 4 — The Partnership Dispute**
- Who: Any age, business has 2–3 co-founders who are misaligned on direction or exit timeline
- What they care about: Clean resolution, speed, confidentiality, getting out without conflict
- What resonates: Structured process that handles complexity, experienced advisors, discretion
- What puts them off: Anything that makes the internal situation more complicated
- Lead angle: Clean exit framing — "a structured process resolves the decision without damaging the business"

---

**Direct Buyer — Personas**

**Persona 1 — The Unaware Seller**
- Who: Successful owner, never thought about selling, has not been approached before
- What they care about: Their business, their team, their reputation — not money yet
- What resonates: Curiosity. Planting the seed that options exist. No pressure framing.
- What puts them off: Anything that sounds like a pitch or a fee
- Lead angle: Curiosity — "not asking you to sell — just worth understanding what the market would say"

**Persona 2 — The Value-Curious Owner**
- Who: Has wondered what the business is worth, may have done informal research, hasn't taken action
- What they care about: Accurate valuation, understanding the process, keeping it confidential
- What resonates: Concrete numbers, speed, no obligation to proceed
- What puts them off: Vague answers, being asked to "send financials" before trust is built
- Lead angle: Valuation framing — "a 30-minute call gives you a realistic sense of what buyers are paying right now"

**Persona 3 — The Previously-Approached Owner**
- Who: Has had a buyer or broker approach before, conversation went nowhere, is now semi-aware
- What they care about: Why this is different, credibility, whether it's worth their time
- What resonates: Direct and credible — "we've done this before in your sector, here's what happened"
- What puts them off: Feeling like the last approach all over again
- Lead angle: Differentiation — "most approaches don't go anywhere because they're exploratory — ours aren't"

### 7. Objections & Reframes

_The most common pushbacks for this client's campaigns and how to neutralise each in a FU or reply context. These feed directly into FU1 and the reply drafting process._

**Sell-Side M&A Advisor**

| Objection | What it usually means | Reframe |
|---|---|---|
| "We're not for sale" | Not actively thinking about it / hasn't considered options | "Understood — most of our clients weren't actively looking when we first spoke. Worth a call to understand your options before you need to make a decision." |
| "Not the right time" | Busy, distracted, not a priority | "Happy to reconnect when the timing is better — when would make more sense?" |
| "I already have an advisor / broker" | May be under contract or in process | "Completely understand — hope it goes well. If anything changes, happy to speak." |
| "Don't need an advisor — I can handle it myself" | Underestimates complexity / overestimates leverage | "Most buyers negotiate hard without representation on the other side. Worth understanding what an advisor costs vs. what it protects." |
| "What's your fee?" | Price sensitivity or testing legitimacy | Answer honestly. No signed mandate, no fee. |
| "Send me more information" | Not ready to commit to a call / wants to assess first | Send one specific thing (case study or brief overview), follow up in 5 days. |

**Direct Buyer**

| Objection | What it usually means | Reframe |
|---|---|---|
| "We're not for sale" | Not actively thinking about it — doesn't mean no interest | "Completely understood — not looking to pressure you. Worth a 30-minute conversation to understand what options exist, even if you never act on them." |
| "What's your offer?" | Sceptical / testing — wants a number before engaging | "Too early to put a number on it without knowing more about the business — that's exactly what the call is for. It would be doing you a disservice to guess." |
| "Are you a broker?" | Confusion about who is reaching out | "We're not a broker or advisory firm — we're the buyer. No fee involved on your side, no obligation." |
| "Not interested" | Genuine disinterest or bad timing | Acknowledge cleanly and leave the door open. Add to nurture if appropriate. |
| "Too busy right now" | Timing issue, not a no | "No problem — when would be a better time? Happy to reconnect in a few months." |
| "Send me more info" | Low trust, wants to assess before committing | Send one short, specific thing. Follow up in 5 days. |
